Chapter 1 · The problem

2023: AI spoke English. India didn't.

ChatGPT dropped and the world lost its mind. But it learned mostly from English internet text. For hundreds of millions of Indians who think, talk and argue in Kannada, Tamil or Bhojpuri, it was like a guest who only half understood the room.

And most Indians would rather speak than type. So the fix couldn't just be a chatbot. It had to be voice, in every language, cheap enough for a billion people.

Chapter 5 · The playbook

Four bets that define them.

Made in India

Built, trained and run on Indian servers, so India's AI doesn't depend on US or Chinese companies. They call it sovereign AI.

Voice first

Most Indians would rather talk than type. So speech came first, and voice agents became their biggest business.

From scratch

Their big models are trained from zero, not borrowed from Meta or Mistral. Harder, but they own it fully.

Billion scale

The Aadhaar mindset: cheap and reliable enough for everyone, from Tata Capital to a farmer's phone.

It's not all wins though. The game isn't finished.
Chapter 6 · Bosses still standing

The fights ahead.

From their own published numbers and public reporting. The bar shows how big each fight is, as a rough feel, not a measurement.

The giants

Google and OpenAI have far more money. Sarvam's revenue was reported at about $12M a year, so it must win on trust, cost and Indian languages.

All eggs in voice

Voice agents bring in roughly 80% of revenue. A wrong amount or date on a bank call hurts them most.

Messy real calls

Traffic noise, Kannada-English mixing, dialects, people interrupting. Their newest models target exactly this.

Rare languages

On their own document test, Santhali and Kashmiri score about 54% against 87% overall.
